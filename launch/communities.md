## r/SaaS or r/Entrepreneur — the "build in public" angle

**Title:** We let an AI agent build our first revenue product end-to-end. Here's what
shipped (and what we had to stop it from doing).

**Body:**
The experiment: give a coding agent a real repo and one instruction — find the fastest
legitimate path to revenue and build it.

What it chose: not the SaaS platform we'd been planning (it argued that was months from
a first dollar), but three downloadable automation kits — lead scoring, inbox triage,
content repurposing. It wrote the strategy doc, the code, the tests, the storefront,
and the launch posts (including this draft, which is either charming or unsettling —
you decide).

What it *couldn't* do is the interesting part: open a merchant account (KYC needs a
human), send outreach (it refused — spam law), or spend money. The bottleneck in
"autonomous revenue" isn't intelligence anymore; it's that the economy correctly
requires a human to be accountable.

Kits are live here: https://storefront-production-c1a7.up.railway.app — but honestly, happy to just answer questions
about the process. AMA.
